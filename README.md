# 💫 About Me:
## Hey, I’m Shankha 👋<br><br>



I started in Android framework development, moved into full-stack development with a backend focus, and now build embedded systems. Somewhere along the way, “Have you checked the logs?” became “Have you checked whether the board has power?”<br><br>
### From Android to embedded<br>
On the software side, I’ve worked with Java, JavaScript, TypeScript, React, Node.js, and the MEAN stack. C, Java, and Python are the languages I’m most comfortable with. I also know the basics of machine learning, which is enough to make me suspicious of a model that scores 100% on its first try.<br><br>These days, I write embedded C and C++ for 8-bit PIC microcontrollers, ESP32, and STM32. I’ve worked with MPLAB X, VS Code, ESP-IDF, and Arduino, and I design circuits and draw schematics in KiCad. I use Proteus, Wokwi, and SimulIDE to test ideas before wiring up the hardware. If the simulator works and the board doesn’t, I check the ground wire before questioning my career choices.<br>I design, build, and manage embedded systems for industrial automation. A project usually starts with “Could we automate this?” I turn that into a system design, a schematic, a BOM, a PCB, firmware, and a working unit I’ve built and tested. Somewhere in there, a two-rupee resistor gets a surprising amount of my attention.<br><br>
### What I do between the sketch and the working system<br><br>

- Figure out what to build. Turn the idea into requirements, decide how the hardware and firmware should work together, and plan the work from design through testing.<br><br>
- Design the hardware. Draw schematics, select components, generate the BOM, and lay out the PCB in KiCad. This is when I try to remember all the places Future Shankha will want to put an oscilloscope probe.<br><br>
- Write the firmware. Develop in embedded C and C++ for 8-bit PIC microcontrollers, ESP32, and STM32. My toolkit includes MPLAB X, VS Code, ESP-IDF, and Arduino, with Proteus, Wokwi, and SimulIDE for simulation.<br><br>
- Build it, bring it up, and test it. Get the board and firmware working together, track down faults, and manage the changes that follow. A single pin change can give the schematic, PCB, firmware, and test plan something to talk about.<br><br>

### Off the bench<br>
For fun, I turn equations from quantum mechanics into simulations, go down astrophysics rabbit holes, and do math voluntarily. I’m also usually listening to music or watching a film. I’m the person who sits through the credits to find out who composed the score.<br><br>If your rabbit hole involves a microcontroller, a wave function, or a film with a great soundtrack, we’ll probably get along.
